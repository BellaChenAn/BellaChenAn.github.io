I started learning Chinese painting when I was eight. But because I kept getting ink all over my face (much to everyone's despair), I switched to Western painting, and at 17 I went on to study it at a specialized art school. In high school, I realized I was more drawn to hands-on, practical work, so at 20 I chose industrial design for my bachelor's degree. There I worked on many projects related to human-computer interaction; I just didn't know yet that it was called HCI.

Luckily, in the summer of 2022 I received a summer internship offer from the AIR lab at Tsinghua University, where I learned HCI alongside a group of brilliant people. After that, I formally moved into HCI research, and I've been here ever since.

My research focuses on children with ADHD. It began when my younger brother was diagnosed with ADHD and I saw how expensive his neurofeedback training at the clinic was. I'm exploring how to make this kind of training more affordable and efficient, and how it can bring more well-being to children with ADHD.
